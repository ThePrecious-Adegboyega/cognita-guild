New to Sudoku? Start here

Three short lessons, from one box to a full grid. Nothing here is timed or scored.

  1. One 3×3 box: fill a single box with 1 to 9, each once. This is the building block of the whole game.
  2. Mini 4×4: the smallest real Sudoku. Every row, column and 2×2 box holds 1 to 4 once.
  3. Full 9×9: take on the Practice Puzzle below with the same ideas.

Practice puzzle

A full 9×9 you can play any time. It is never scored.

The Rules

  1. Fill the 9×9 grid so every row, every column and every 3×3 box contains the digits 1 to 9 once.
  2. Numbers already in the grid are fixed clues. You cannot change them.
  3. Every puzzle has exactly one solution. You never need to guess.

How to use the board

  1. Tap a cell, then tap a number on the pad (or type on a keyboard).
  2. A red number is wrong and adds one to your mistake count. Erase it and try again.
  3. Notes switches to pencil marks: small candidate numbers in a cell.
  4. Erase clears the selected cell. Hint fills the selected cell correctly, and is listed in your result.
  5. Tap a number to highlight every matching digit on the board.

Practice game

Play White against the House computer. Pick a level below. Nothing here is scored.

How to play

  1. Tap one of your pieces. Green dots show every legal square it can go to. Green rings mark captures.
  2. Tap a dot to move. The computer replies straight away.
  3. Your king glows red when in check. You must escape it.
  4. Pawns reaching the last rank become a Queen automatically.
  5. Castling, en passant, checkmate and stalemate all work as in standard chess.
  6. Undo takes back your last move and the reply. Every undo is listed in your result.

Chess for complete beginners

Two armies of 16 pieces face each other. Goal: checkmate, which means trapping the enemy King so it cannot escape capture. Learn each piece below. Green dots are squares it can move to, and ✕ marks squares where a pawn captures.

Setting up the board

  1. Each player has a white-square corner on their right hand.
  2. Back row, left to right: Rook, Knight, Bishop, Queen, King, Bishop, Knight, Rook. The Queen stands on her own colour, the King beside her.
  3. Pawns fill the row in front. White always moves first.

Special moves

  1. Castling: King moves two squares toward a Rook, and that Rook jumps to the other side of him. Allowed only if neither has moved, the squares between are empty, and the King is not in check or crossing an attacked square. In this game, tap the King, then the square two steps over.
  2. En passant: if an enemy pawn jumps two squares and lands beside your pawn, you may capture it as if it had moved one square. You must do it right away.
  3. Promotion: a pawn reaching the far rank becomes another piece. Here it becomes a Queen.

Check, checkmate, stalemate

  1. Check: your King is attacked. You must escape by moving it, blocking, or capturing the attacker.
  2. Checkmate: in check with no escape. Game over.
  3. Stalemate: not in check, but no legal move. It is a draw.

First tips

  1. Before every move, ask: what is the computer threatening?
  2. Control the centre with pawns, then bring out Knights and Bishops.
  3. Castle early to protect your King.
  4. Do not trade a bigger piece for a smaller one. Use the values above.
  5. Start on Easy in practice. Use Undo freely while learning.
